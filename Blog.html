How to Fix Your Inbound Operations

July 2026 • By Christopher Riley II

The Biggest Problems

Inbound is one of the most critical aspects of your warehouse operations. When it runs well everything downstream flows well. When it breaks down everything downstream suffers. The biggest problems I see are driver check-in complications, trucks showing up virtually in your Yard management system but not physically on site, checked into the wrong door, or still showing as on-site from a previous visit. When your yard is a mess your whole schedule falls apart before the first pallet arrives the floor. The second issue is slow unloads caused by role confusion. When your unloaders are also responsible for labeling both processes suffer. Associates end up moving toward the pallets instead of the pallets moving toward them. The third issue is non-standard putaway pratices and double stacking, placing product in unrelated zones, skipping condensing when slots are tight. This throws off your warehouse management system algorithm and causes the system to place new freight in the wrong locations.

What Actually Works

For driver check-in you should map out your arrival schedule and build in at least one hour buffer between loads. Stagger your trucks, one per hour instead of multiple at once. This gives your team time to properly check in each driver, assign them a door or a waiting area, processes the unload and keep your yard data clean. For unloads you should split your roles. Station your labelers in the receiving area and have unloaders should bring pallets to them. Receivers stay receivers, labelers stay labelers. The product moves to the people not the other way around. For putaway the rule is simple, you need to condense before you expand to other zone. If a slot is tight combine two partial pallets into one before you start placing freight in random zones. Always update your WMS in real time. If your system does not know where product is it will send your team to the wrong place every time./p>

A Real Example

When I worked at Walmart I inherited a situation where my inbound team had ran out of slots to receive some trucks coming from another site that was under construction. I walked the floor and found 10 locations where we could condense two pallets into one, freeing up 10 slots immediately. While that was happening I had my inventory team complete an extra sweep of the floor while the inventory lead audited our virtual inventory versus physical inventory counts. We were off by 500 packages. The reason was outbound picks from the previous week had never been updated in the WMS so the system was treating that product as still on hand and routing new pallets into zones that were already allocated. Once we closed that gap the algorithm started placing freight correctly into open and near slots, our slot availability opened up, and we received every truck on schedule. The lesson here is your WMS is only as smart as the data you give it. Keep your counts clean and your putaway standard and your inbound operation will run itself and make yourleft so much easier.

How to Get Promoted from Shift Manager to Operations Manager

July 2026 • By Christopher Riley II

The Biggest Difference

As a shift manager your leading associates. As an ops manager you are now leading leaders. You are now responsible for your shift managers, their teams, and the results of your entire department. You now spend less time on the floor, more time in meetings, planning for volume, planning headcounts, managing cost, and running process improvement projects. You are the one accountable when the numbers are off and you are the one who has to fix it.

What Actually gets you promoted

The biggest thing I did was learn. Not just the job that im assigned to do but the math behind it. I studied our SOPs, understood how rates and metrics were calculated, and I learned what actually drove performance at each level of the operation. I also invested in myself outside of work. I got my Lean Six Sigma Green Belt, my OSHA 30, and started working toward my bachelor's degree(Shout out to ASU!). I signed up for leadership classes on budgets and new technology being introduced on site. None of that was required, I did it because I knew it would separate me. Beyond certifications I raised my hand for the hard assignments. During my tenure at Amazon, I assisted with three site turnarounds, took on projects to improve pick rates, reduce driver returns, and fix inbound receiving. I also focused on developing my team and had five associates I promoted to shift manager roles under my leadership. I would say nothing gets you noticed faster than producing other leaders.

Mistakes That Keep Shift Managers Stuck

The most common one is staying comfortable. Things change fast in warehouse operations and if you are still saying we always did it this way you are already behind your goals. The managers who get promoted are the ones who adapt and bring new ideas to the table daily. The second mistake is letting your metrics slip. Missing your rates consistently as a shift manager tells leadership you are not ready for more responsibility. Own your numbers every for every single shift. The third mistake is poor morale on your team. Leadership watches how your associates respond to you. If your shift has high turnover, low engagement, and constant complaints that will end your promotion track.

A Moment That Defined My Career

Early in my management career I had to cover two shifts alone, running both over night inbound and daytime outbound simultaneously while handling some of my ops manager's responsibilities. My OM and my counterpart on day shift were both out with COVID at the same time. That was one of the hardest stretches of my career. I had to prioritize speed, communicate constantly to my team leads and associates, and keep both operations moving without dropping the ball on either side. I did not have all the answers but I stayed calm, leaned on my team help me out, and get through it. That experience taught me more about operational management than any class I ever took. When your back is against the wall and you figure it out anyway that is when you know you are ready for the next level, take care of your team and they will take care of you.

How to Improve Morale for Floor Associates

July 2026 • By Christopher Riley II

What Kills Morale

Treating your associates like numbers. When a manager only shows up to write someone up or push a rate the team will notices. People will do the bare minimum for a manager they do not care for or respect and go above and beyond for the one who genuinely cares. Not recognizing hard work is the second killer. If your top performers see that effort goes unrewarded they will stop performing.

What Actually Works

Recognition does not have to be expensive. team Swag, prizes, and shoutouts go a long way. Give your best people new challenges. designate problem solver roles, create shadowing opportunities, and rotate people through different roles so nobody gets burned out. Put your weight behind your team. Advocate for transfers, recommend people for promotions out loud to leadership. When your team knows you are in their corner they will run through a wall for you.

What I Did That Made a Difference

At Amazon I scored an 86 out of 100 on my engagement survey while my division average was 72. I got there by showing up for my team every single shift. I made rounds to all 100 of my associates and had real conversations about their day and their life — not rates or metrics. I ate with my team instead of in the manager's office and personally thanked every person before they left

Turning Around a Struggling Site

During a site turnaround assignement I spent my first few days talking to all 200 associates. The feedback was consistent — managers only showed up when someone neded a write up, no one knew the shift plan, and nobody could tell them how to move up. I fixed all three. I had managers talk to their teams daily, disscuss a clear shift plan every day, and gave associates a real roadmap from associate to general manager. Morale is very important. It is an ops problem and it starts with leadership.
